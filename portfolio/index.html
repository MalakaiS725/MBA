Capstone: Premier Restoration Hawai‘i

Premier turns away one job in four, in a slow month. It is short on crews, not on calls. That single fact kills the growth case we wrote in October, because more leads would only mean more refusals. The marketing budget is not an instrument for raising volume at this company. It is the only lever on which work arrives — and a commercial job returns $3,143 more gross profit than a residential one. Shifting the mix ten points is worth $31,430 per hundred jobs, with no extra crew, no extra job and no extra dollar of spend.

This thesis replaced our own. A recorded client interview on 23 November overturned three things we had built on: that Premier used pre-loss agreements, that residential against commercial was the right margin axis, and that the problem was demand. The documents below are the rebuild.

A note on the figures. The capstone deliverable set at the top of this page runs on figures Premier Restoration supplied directly — a written data return and a recorded interview on 23 November 2026. Job values, margins, close rates, spend and the refusal rate are the client's own. Two inputs are ours rather than theirs, the blended gross margin and the starting job mix; both are flagged in the deck, the dossier and the model's source log, and the sensitivity analysis shows the recommendation holds without them.

Earlier coursework on this page predates that data. Budgets and volumes in the weekly artefacts are modelling figures, and each document says so in its own method note. The Oura work uses publicly available traffic estimates and published pricing, not the company's own analytics.
